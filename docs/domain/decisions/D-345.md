**REQ-deploy-1s counts from the instant the doorbell heard GitHub's signed push of the merge to main, on the engine's own millisecond clock, not from GitHub's push timestamp (LANDING-CLOCK; asked by the Torre 19/11, decided by the session under D-325).** GitHub's push timestamp has whole seconds: a clock as coarse as the 1 s target can neither prove nor refuse it, so the clock itself would decide the verdict. The doorbell stamps each landing word it receives (heard, milliseconds) on a clock the engine controls, and only a word signed by GitHub's push hook counts as a landing. That is the first instant any part of the engine can know main moved; GitHub's delivery of the hook before it is not a step a deploy design can act on, and it stays visible as the push -> heard step of scripts/deploy status. A merge with no signed word falls back to the push timestamp and is marked from: push, so a run never passes on a finer clock it did not have. Runs recorded from the push stay in the sample until they age out (7 days). Revert path: drop heard_at from record_live, and every run is timed from the push again.
