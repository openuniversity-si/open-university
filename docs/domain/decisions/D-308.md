**The infrastructure adapts to parameters and never adapts by removing one (Daniel 04/11 12:40 BRT: "Uma coisa é não limitar o comportamento, outra coisa é não permitir o comportamento. [...] A fila poder se adaptar a diferentes tipos de prioridade, isso sim é abstraído. [...] uma coisa é ser adaptável a diferentes parâmetros, outra coisa é você simplesmente tirar o parâmetro, bicho."; refines D-306).** The boundary of D-306 takes choices out of the infrastructure, never capability: every mechanism of the work and log schemas keeps its parameters, and its default is a value that whoever uses the infrastructure changes, never the absence of the parameter. A queue orders by the policy it names (fifo by default, rank, deadline or another module plugged in) with each job's rank, not_before and deadline; a job carries its retry policy (one attempt by default; 10 s first wait, factor 2, at most 10 min; failed and timed_out ask for another attempt, the error types it names never do), its route (an exact executor, or a selector the capacity decides), its dependencies, its exclusive key and memoize; limits and meter prices are rows whose values come from outside. A queue without ordering, or a failure without retry, would be the infrastructure choosing a behaviour, and this rule refuses it. The first migration, infra/db/migrations/0001_work_log.sql (the annex DDL of the phase-1 target architecture), implements it. Proof: scripts/tests/test_infra_db.py applies the migration to the toolchain's PostgreSQL 18.6 and runs the phase-1 acceptance test on it: one queue function ordering by priority, by arrival and by deadline as each queue's policy says; retry with growing waits, the default of one attempt and a non-retryable error; routing that skips an executor with no slot; a money limit by the token price; and every default read back from the catalog as a value. Revert path: revert this record and the migration; nothing applies the schema yet.
