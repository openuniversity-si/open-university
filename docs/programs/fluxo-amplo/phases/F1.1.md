# F1.1 — one serial lane at its ceiling (target 200 counted commits/day)

Phase 1, first sub-phase of the ladder 200 … 1,000 (solucao.md). Serial, by order (RC-002 approved by Daniel,
31/10 15:23 BRT: F1.1 and F1.2 serial).

## Auditoria
Why does the work exist? The lane's cycle is 70 % authoring and 30 % waiting on gate and train, paid once per PR
(audit findings 3, 5). Why does the lane wait at all? The session ends its turn after a push and resumes on a result;
nothing in the flow asks it to keep authoring while the gate and train run. Why is the wait paid per 5 commits? Plans
are written small. So the ideal lane never idles: the next PR's commits are authored while the previous PR's gate and
train run, and each PR is filled to its plan. The metric must also be readable per lane: nothing yet prints wait per PR,
authoring per commit and lanes per day, so the claim "serial ceiling 216/day" cannot be checked per phase.

## Plano
1. **Redesign (N4): the pipelined lane.** After a PR's head is pushed and `pr.ready` is emitted, branch the next PR from
   that head and keep authoring; when the previous PR merges the new branch carries only its own commits (git sees the
   merged ones in the base). The session checks the queue once per PR, with no polling loop.
2. **Class (N3): PRs are filled to their plan** (8 items, each a cohesive commit with its own `Plan: N`), so the per-PR
   wait is spread over more commits; guards (D-137) decide which commits count.
3. **Point: the measurements the phase needs, in `scripts/flow-metrics`:** wait per PR and authoring per commit, lanes
   per day, and a durability guard by operations (lines a commit added that one of the next K integrated commits
   rewrites). Each is a commit with its test.

## Fechamento (D-133): did the goal improve or only the number?
**Number:** 8.3 counted commits an hour is 200 a day; the last three hours ran at 14.7 an hour (27, 3, 14: 44 commits), and the last 12 hours at 8.4 an hour (101). The 24 h total is 160, because the first hours of the window were the slow ones. `scripts/flow-metrics --hourly 12 --rolling`. Guards did not move (split 2.6 %, tangled 9.5 %, fixup 0.2 %); plan-linked rose from 63.6 % to 64.4 %.

**Goal, partly.** What really changed in the flow: (1) a lane no longer idles on the train: stale gates are dropped (D-141, D-145), a stacked branch pushes (D-142), the runner refreshes (D-150); (2) the wait per PR fell from 14 to 25 minutes (medians 14.4 and 25) to about 1 minute (gate 9 to 42 s, train 44 to 48 s); (3) the base is checked by the real gate in the background (D-153, D-154, D-157), which found two breaks the scoped gate could not see. What did not change: the real gate on the e2-micro is still not under 10 s, and the 1-minute merge exists because the regime of exception (D-151) gives the gate as green; the safe point behind each commit is therefore vouched for by the shadow gate and the lane's own local gate, not by the train. The number is real and the guards hold, but the part of the speed-up that comes from the bypass is not an improvement of the flow until the real gate is fast (lane 2, D-155).
