## Auditoria
Why does the work exist? F1.2 took authoring to 99 s a commit and the lap to one command, and the pace followed (23.3 an hour over
three hours). One lane writing at 99 s could land 36 an hour; it lands 23: the difference is overhead the lane still pays per PR
(open it, its body, the base, the plan, the wait for the train) and the minutes between PRs. Two lanes multiply what is left. The
question for this phase is therefore not "can the flow take more" (the train merges about one PR every 45 s, about 80 PRs an hour,
unmeasured above 10) but "do the guards hold when two lanes write at once": the prose share (`docsonly`, D-164) is above its start value, and a
second lane on the gate (docs and registry heavy) is where it can rise.

## Plano
1. **Redesign** (done in F1.2): the lap is one command (`lane-ship --create`, D-245), the train and the shadow gate are one loop
   (`train-loop --shadow`, D-157), the status is one command (`lane-status`, D-156, D-163).
2. **Class**: the guards at the point of writing, not after: `--admit` (empty, fixup, split, rework in the range), the reader check and the plan
   check before the gate (D-159, D-162, D-246), the guard ratios of the last day on the status line.
3. **Point**: the hourly pace per lane, so one lane's dip is seen (a lane is its branch family: `lane-status` per `lane2-*` and the others).
